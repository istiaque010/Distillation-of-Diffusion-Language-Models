Distillation of Diffusion Language Models

Can a student learn to write in 8 passes what its teacher needs 32 for?

Project 7 tutorial

Project 3 trained a small masked diffusion language model (MDLM-13.9M). It writes a story by filling blanks over many passes (calls of the model). Projects 5 and 6 kept it frozen and improved the decoder. Project 7 changes the model instead: we train a student to imitate what the slow teacher does over several passes, so that the student needs fewer passes.

This page explains every step: what we did, why, what result we got, and how that result told us what to do next. You don't need to read Projects 1–6 first. Idea 1 recaps what you need.

The one question

Can a diffusion language model be distilled so that it needs fewer denoising steps while keeping the quality of the slow teacher?

The one idea behind everything

teacher: 32 small steps  ──►  record what it fixes in each step  ──►  student: learn to make 4 of them in ONE step
fewer passes = faster.   The question is whether the student's stories stay as good.

The short answer

Partly, and mostly through sharpening. After 26 minutes of training on a CPU, the student writes stories at 8 passes as well as the teacher at 16, 20% better than the teacher at 8, and 3.4× faster than the teacher at 32. The teacher's guidance clearly helps beyond extra training on real text. But the student's stories are less varied, and simply turning the teacher's temperature down to 0.8, with no training at all, gives even lower perplexity with more variety. Most of what the student learned is to be more confident, not to tell better stories.

The pipeline (the order we did things in)

Frozen teacher, same tools→ 6,300 teacher trajectories→ Write predictions first→ Pilot: learning rate too high→ 9 students × 1,500 updates→ Pick the best on validation→ Test: 16 systems × 1,200 stories→ Speed · ablations · error review

Idea 1 The teacher writes by filling blanks, pass by pass

The model from Project 3, unchanged and frozen.

A language model gives a probability to every possible token (a word or part of a word; our tokenizer knows 8,192). Project 3's MDLM-13.9M is a masked diffusion model: it learned to guess tokens hidden behind a [MASK] blank, using the words on both sides.

To write a story we give it the prompt followed by blanks (a window of 256 tokens). One pass gives a guess for every blank at once. The decoder (Project 6's best) fixes some of those guesses for good, at random positions but never two neighbours together, and calls the model again. With a budget of K passes it fixes about 1/K of the blanks each time.

pass 0:  Once upon a time ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢
pass 1:  Once upon a time ▢ ▢ girl ▢ ▢ ▢ park ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢ ▢
pass 2:  Once upon a time ▢ little girl ▢ ▢ to park ▢ She ▢ ▢ ▢
  ...    (32 passes for the "slow" teacher)

a painter who adds a few brush strokes per look at the canvas. More looks = a better picture, but each look takes time.

Idea 2 Why fewer passes make worse stories

Tokens fixed in the same pass can't see each other.

In one pass every blank's token is drawn independently, from what the model saw before the pass. With 32 passes, ~7 tokens are fixed per pass and each one sees almost everything already written. With 8 passes, ~30 tokens are drawn "blind" to each other per pass; with 4, ~60. They can disagree: "The dog … she … his cat".

Teacher, no retraining: scorer perplexity by number of passes (test, 1,200 stories each)

Real. Lower is better (Idea 9 explains the measure). Real children's stories score 4.5.

Going from 32 to 8 passes makes the teacher's stories 52% worse; to 4 passes, 2.8× worse. That is the gap distillation tries to close.

Idea 3 What distillation is

A student learns from a teacher's answers, not only from the data.

Knowledge distillation trains a student model on the outputs of a teacher. The teacher's output is richer than the data: instead of "the right word is park", it says "park 40%, garden 25%, woods 10%…". These are called soft targets.

Here we use it for step distillation: the student is not smaller (it starts as an exact copy of the teacher), it is taught to do in one pass what the teacher does in several. So every difference comes from training, not from size. (One extra student, kd_small, also drops 2 of the 6 layers.)

teacher:  state ─pass─► ─pass─► ─pass─► ─pass─► state 4 passes later
student:  state ─────────── one pass ──────────► (should land in the same place)

Idea 4 Trajectories: what the teacher did, pass by pass

Record the slow teacher once, learn from it many times.

We let the teacher write 6,300 stories with 32 passes and stored, for every token, the pass at which it was fixed. That is the whole trajectory: since fixed tokens never change, "the state after pass s" = all tokens fixed at a pass ≤ s, blanks elsewhere.

A student with S passes makes one pass where the teacher made 32/S. So before student pass j+1 it sees the teacher's state after pass a = 32·j/S, and it must fix what the teacher fixed in passes a+1 … b (the span, b = 32·(j+1)/S). Everything still blank after pass b is the rest.

Blue = the span: what this one student pass must fix, with the teacher's token as the answer. Grey boxes = the rest: still blank afterwards; here the student learns the teacher's probabilities.

Idea 5 The loss: KL divergence, temperature T and weight λ

How "be like the teacher" becomes a number to minimise.

Cross-entropy (CE) punishes the student for giving a low probability to the right token. KL divergence measures how far the student's whole probability list is from the teacher's: 0 if identical. We use KL(teacher ‖ student): the student must cover everything the teacher thinks possible.

The temperature T flattens both lists before comparing them (T = 2 makes the 2nd, 3rd… choices matter more); the loss is multiplied by T² to keep its size. λ mixes the teacher signal with real text:

L_KD = span positions: CE(student, teacher's token)  +  rest positions: T²·KL(teacher_later/T ‖ student/T)
L_CE = CE(student, real TinyStories token) on real masked windows
L    = λ·L_KD + (1 − λ)·L_CE          λ = 0: no teacher at all · λ = 1: teacher only

One subtle point, worked out before training: a student that is an exact copy of the teacher already predicts the teacher's distribution at the same state, so "copy the teacher at the same state" teaches it nothing. Only the teacher's later state (after more passes) contains something new.

Idea 6 The fair control: training without the teacher

Is it the teacher, or just more training?

Any extra training might help. So we trained a CE-only student exactly like the others (same start, same 1,500 updates, same states, same seed) but with λ = 0: real text only. The teacher's contribution is KD student vs CE-only student, not KD student vs teacher.

A mistake we caught: the first CE-only run used learning rate 1e-4 and made every held-out number worse (the teacher was already at its best and got knocked off). We lowered it to 3e-5 for every run. Now the CE-only student stays flat: a fair control that neither helps nor hurts on held-out loss.

Idea 7 The sharpening trap

A cheaper way to look better.

The teacher's later predictions are more decided (more words are known). A student trained on them may simply learn to be more confident everywhere. But confidence can be had for free: sample the teacher with temperature 0.8 (divide its scores by 0.8 before drawing), which favours likely tokens. Likely tokens = lower perplexity, but also less variety.

8 passes, test set: fluency and variety side by side

Real. Perplexity: lower = more fluent. Distinct-2: share of different word pairs, higher = more varied.

The distilled student beats the plain teacher on perplexity but loses variety, and the temperature-0.8 teacher does better on both. We predicted the opposite (H8) and were wrong.

Idea 8 Watching training

Fixed held-out checks every 250 updates; update 0 = the untouched teacher.

Held-out loss during training (lower is better)

Real. span CE = how well the student guesses the token the teacher will fix in the next span. real text CE = how well it guesses hidden words of real stories, masked like a 4/8/16-pass decoder. The 4-layer student (off by default; click it in the legend) starts much higher because it lost two layers.

The KD students get better at the thing they are taught (span CE falls), and worse at real text the more weight the teacher gets (λ = 1 loses most). They are moving toward the teacher's own style, away from real stories.

Idea 9 Quality vs passes

Scorer perplexity: how surprised an independent model is by a story.

We can't read 20,000 stories, so a separate, bigger model (TinyStories-33M, the scorer) reads each one. Perplexity = how surprised it is; lower = more fluent and predictable. Every system writes for the same 400 test prompts × 3 seeds, so comparisons are paired, with a 95% confidence interval (the bar on each point).

Scorer perplexity by passes

Real. Log scales. Point at a marker for the numbers.

The KD student's curve sits a full "step" below the teacher's: 8 student passes ≈ 16 teacher passes, and 16 student passes beat 32 teacher passes. The CE-only student barely moves.

Idea 10 Speed: it's just the number of passes

A student pass costs the same as a teacher pass.

Median time per story, batch 1, CPU (40 stories each)

Real. 24 CPU threads, fp32.

8 passes are 3.4× faster than 32 (86% of the ideal 4×; each pass also has a fixed cost for choosing and drawing tokens). The 4-layer student is another 1.3× faster per pass, but its stories are worse than the untouched teacher's.

Idea 11 Reading the stories

Same test prompt, same seed, five systems

We also had 80 stories (20 prompts × 4 systems) labelled blind on a 1–5 rubric and seven failure types. The labels were written by an AI assistant, not by people, and are not human-verified.

What and why

Lessons learned (useful for any ML project)

  • Always build the boring control. Without the CE-only student we could not tell "the teacher helped" from "more training helped". Without the temperature-0.8 teacher we would have reported distillation as a clear win.
  • Ask what else could explain the gain. Lower perplexity can come from better stories or from safer, more predictable ones. Look at a variety measure next to every fluency measure.
  • Think before you train. Working out on paper that same-state targets give a teacher-copy no signal saved a wasted design, and the ablation confirmed it.
  • Check the first run before launching nine. The pilot showed lr 1e-4 hurt; nine runs at that rate would have wasted four hours.
  • Loss is not quality. The student that predicts real text worst writes the stories the scorer likes best.
  • Thresholds depend on how you measure. Distinct-2 over 100 stories is lower than over 50, so a rule set on validation can fail on test without anything changing.
  • Write the predictions first. Two of eight were wrong, and they are the most interesting results.

Glossary

The models
Token
a word or piece of a word; our tokenizer has 8,192.
Masked diffusion model (MDLM)
a model trained to guess hidden tokens using words on both sides; writes by filling blanks over several passes.
Pass (NFE)
one call of the model; NFE = number of function evaluations.
Teacher / student
the slow, frozen model / the model trained to imitate it with fewer passes.
Frozen
weights never change.
Fine-tuning
continuing to train an already-trained model, usually with a small learning rate.
Distillation
Knowledge distillation (KD)
training a student on a teacher's outputs.
Step distillation
teaching the student to do in one pass what the teacher does in several.
Trajectory
the sequence of states the teacher goes through while writing; stored as the pass at which each token was fixed.
State, span, rest
what the student sees / what one student pass must fix / what is still blank after it.
Soft targets
the teacher's full probability list instead of a single right answer.
KL divergence
how far one probability list is from another; 0 when identical.
Temperature T
divide scores by T before turning them into probabilities: T > 1 flattens, T < 1 sharpens.
λ (lambda_KD)
weight of the teacher loss; 1 − λ goes to real text.
CE-only student
the same training with λ = 0: the control without a teacher.
Sharpening
making a model more confident (likely tokens even likelier); lowers perplexity and variety.
Learning rate
how big each training step is; too big knocks a trained model off its best point.
Measures and statistics
Scorer perplexity
how surprised an independent model (TinyStories-33M) is by a story; lower = more fluent.
Distinct-2
share of different word pairs in a group of stories; higher = more varied.
Repetitive story
a story that repeats long chunks (Project 4's measure).
Paired bootstrap
resample prompts many times, comparing two systems on the same prompts, to get a 95% interval.
Validation (dev) / test
prompts for making choices / prompts used once for the final answer.
Ablation
change one setting, keep everything else, see what moves.
AI labels
judgements written by an AI assistant, not by people; disclosed wherever used.
